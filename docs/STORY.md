# The Bicycle, beat sheet

Logline: A small, dented household robot spends one day searching the back streets of Kyoto for his
elderly owner's missing bicycle, helped by a suspicious shiba and a useless network of street cats.

Runtime target 3:30. No dialogue. Late September.

## Structure

1. **Quiet morning (0:00 to 0:31).** A cat stares at us. Dawn on a Higashiyama lane. An empty
   rectangle of stone beside a machiya door where something used to stand. The robot wakes, opens the
   door, sees the empty spot. The old woman comes out humming, sees it, stops humming, looks for three
   seconds, goes back inside.
2. **The decision (0:31 to 0:45).** Alone in the lane, the robot puts a bicycle glyph on his face and
   rolls off. The shiba, tied outside a shop, watches him go.
3. **The search (0:45 to 1:43).** Shrine wall cats give him a direction. The shiba follows, standoff,
   then the shiba remembers seeing the bike leave and becomes the guide. The black cat detours them to
   Nishiki market for fish. The bobtail is sitting on a child's yellow cap: a clue. Kamo river, people
   evenly spaced, the robot's battery at two bars. The elder cat falls asleep mid-answer; the shiba gets
   the scent off the cap.
4. **Dusk and the low point (1:43 to 2:06).** Pontocho lanterns come on one by one; rain begins; the
   shiba runs ahead and disappears. The robot's battery blinks at one bar. He stops under an awning by a
   glowing vending machine. His face glyph goes out. Only rain moves. Then the soaked shiba returns and
   drops the bicycle's bell at his wheel.
5. **The turn and the finding (2:06 to 2:32).** Wet streets, lanterns, a small vermilion gate, the cats
   on the walls watching them pass. An alley behind a shuttered shop: the bicycle on its side and a boy
   on a step with a scraped knee. The robot puts the cap back on the boy's head. The shiba licks the
   knee.
6. **The return (2:32 to 3:02).** The boy pushes the bicycle home, robot beside him, shiba ahead. The
   old woman is waiting on the doorstep in the dark. The boy bows. She puts her hand on the saddle, pats
   it: get on. She holds the saddle and runs behind him down the lane, and lets go. He doesn't fall.
   The melody plays in full for the first time.
7. **The photograph (3:02 to 3:24).** Match cut to the framed photo in the entrance: her, thirty years
   younger, holding the saddle for a small girl on the same bicycle, same lane. The robot on the step at
   zero battery watches the bicycle come home, and his screen goes dark. Her hand lays a folded cloth over
   his dented shoulder. The boy rings the bell: clack, clack, then it rings.
8. **Morning again, credits (3:24 to end).** The spot is not empty. The robot charges beside the
   bicycle, the shiba asleep against his wheel, the grey cat on the wall. Hand-lettered credits.

## Why it should land

The film never tells you the bicycle matters. It shows you a woman look at a patch of stone for three
seconds and stop humming. Everything the robot does after that is him trying to get the hum back. When
she runs behind the boy, we understand she has done this before, and the photograph confirms it without
a word. The bell working again is small enough to be believable and large enough to feel like grace.
